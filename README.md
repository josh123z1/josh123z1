## 📊 Featured Projects

### 🗄️ [Online Bookstore Database & Analytics](https://github.com/YOUR-USERNAME/online-bookstore-sql)
`SQL` `SQLite` `Python` `Power BI`

I designed a 9-table database for an online bookstore, normalised it to third normal form and built it in SQLite. Keys plus UNIQUE and CHECK constraints enforce the business rules. I generated realistic test data with Python (Faker) and wrote SQL validation queries to catch duplicates, missing values and orphan records. The business questions were then answered in SQL and Power BI dashboards.

### 💳 [Loan Default Prediction](https://github.com/YOUR-USERNAME/loan-default-prediction)
`Python` `PyTorch` `scikit-learn`

There were about 50,000 loans and only 15% defaulted. I handled the imbalance with SMOTE and undersampling and compared neural-network models on recall and ROC-AUC instead of accuracy. On held-out data, the final model **caught about two-thirds of defaulters**. I grouped borrowers into low, medium and high-risk bands so a lender could act on each group.

### 🏦 [Predicting Business Credit Applicants](https://github.com/YOUR-USERNAME/business-credit-prediction) *(group project)*
`Python` `scikit-learn` `CRISP-DM`

The goal was to rank 100,000 bank customers by how likely they were to apply for business credit, when only 10% actually did. We compared six models and chose logistic regression, which **caught 77% of applicants (ROC-AUC 0.855)**. Contacting the top 30–40% of the ranked list would reach **over 80% of real applicants**.

### 📈 [Household Spending & Podcast Audience Analysis](https://github.com/YOUR-USERNAME/spending-podcast-regression)
`R` `tidyverse` `ggplot2`

I ran regression on 4,000+ households and about 5,400 podcasts. Food spending was driven by housing, alcohol and debt payments, not by household size. For podcasts, the number of reviews mattered but the average score didn't, and **genre alone explained 56% of the variation in daily listens**.
